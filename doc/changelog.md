# Release notes

## Unreleased

- Name the links in a table's cells for VoiceOver on Mac, reach one that is a hidden label, and find a cell's links from the page. A table cell is a text of its own, which VoiceOver reads after interacting with the cell's row, and there the links were still AppKit's, named by their characters. A link that is a picture was "hyperlink" with no name for Find Next Link, whatever its `alt` said; a link that is a hidden label was not reached; reading the cell by word VoiceOver said nothing at a picture or a label in a link; and from the page's text Find Next Link said "Link not found" where a page's only links are in a table. A cell now gives VoiceOver the reader's own link elements, as the text around the table does. Inside a cell Find Next Link says a link's name and "hyperlink": a picture's words and that it is an image, "image" alone for decoration that is all a link holds, a hidden label's words, and a link's words without the decoration beside them. Reading by word and by line VoiceOver says "hyperlink" and the name at a picture or a label in a link, and Control-Option-Space follows the link from there, from Find Next Link and from a word of its line. The page lists the links of its cells on screen among its own, so Find Next Link from the page goes through them in reading order, in pages and in continuous scroll. A link of words in a cell is read as its words, as everywhere on the Mac.
- Offer the page turn to VoiceOver on Mac where its cursor is, and bring VoiceOver to the first column after a turn made in the second. A listener on Mac had no page action. VoiceOver's Actions menu (Control-Option-Command-Space) held "show menu" alone, on a column and inside its text, in pages and in continuous scroll: the reader named Next Page and Previous Page on its SwiftUI surface, which wraps a view that is not an accessibility element on Mac, and neither they nor its scroll action reached any element of the window. Each text area now offers the pair itself, Next Page and Previous Page in pages and Scroll Forward and Scroll Backward in continuous scroll, which are the names of the host's buttons and follow a change of flow made with the reader open. Choosing one turns the spread or scrolls one viewport less a line, and VoiceOver is then on the first line of the new page or screen. It is a turn asked for by name, as a line's is on iPhone and iPad: selected text does not hold it back, it fails at either end of the book, and it is there under `EPUBPageControls.hostOnly`, where a listener had the host's buttons and nothing else. The surface's two named actions and its scroll action are gone on Mac. VoiceOver now says "actions available" before its hint for a text area, once after it arrives at a column or enters its text, and not after each line. In a spread of two columns a page turn made in the second, by a page key or by the action, left VoiceOver on the first line of the new second column, and the new first column was passed over: keyboard focus now goes to the first column after such a turn, and VoiceOver reads from there. Not changed: in continuous scroll a step made straight after VoiceOver enters the text, before it has moved inside it, is undone by VoiceOver going back to the start of the text, with the action as with a page key; and a link that VoiceOver's cursor rests on keeps its own actions, without the pair. Nothing changes on iPhone or iPad ([#447](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/447)).
- Go on from the scroll bar to what follows the reader, and back from it to the last line, when VoiceOver reads past the end of a book in continuous scroll on iPhone and iPad, and stop the text moving each time VoiceOver comes to the scroll bar. After the book's last line VoiceOver read "Vertical scroll bar" and then the first line on screen again, and read down the screen a second time; moving back from the bar it went to the control before the text, and a touch on the bar anywhere in the book left it the same two neighbours. UIKit gives VoiceOver a scroll view's bar after the view's last element, but keeps the bar among the scroll view's header views, and asked for the items each side of the bar it answered as for a header: the first line listed, and whatever precedes the scroll view. The reader now hides UIKit's bar from assistive technology and lists an item of its own in the bar's place, after its last line. The item says what the bar says, in UIKit's words and language, and scrolls by a page when adjusted, by asking the bar; as one of the reader's own items its neighbours are the last line and what follows the reader. It also lies inside the reading area's insets, where the bar reaches under them, so UIKit no longer takes the text 88 points back as VoiceOver's cursor comes to the bar, from the text or from a control below the reader ([#342](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/342)).
- Move to the next heading from a link with VoiceOver on Mac. With VoiceOver's cursor on a link, where Find Next Link (Control-Option-Command-L) leaves it, its move to the next heading (Control-Option-Command-H) said "Heading not found" in pages and in continuous scroll, though headings followed. A link is an item of its own inside the text on Mac, and from an item VoiceOver looks for a heading among the text's other items: it does not ask the reader's Headings rotor, which serves the move from a place in the text. Each heading on screen is now such an item too, with its level. In continuous scroll the move from a link goes to the book's next heading and scrolls to it, and VoiceOver says the level, "heading level 2 Link target reached", which it does not say when it comes to a heading from the text. In pages the move goes to the next heading on the page. When none follows the link VoiceOver goes back to the page's first heading, as Find Next Link begins again with the first link past the last, and on a page with no heading it still says "Heading not found". VoiceOver's cursor is then on the heading and not in the text: one move right (Control-Option-Right Arrow) returns to the text, where the move by heading goes through the whole book. The rotor's list of headings (Control-Option-U) was already offered from a link and is as it was ([#431](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/431)).
- Read what stands in a table cell to VoiceOver on Mac: a rule, a formula, a picture or a hidden label. A cell is a text area of its own there, which VoiceOver reaches by interacting with the cell's row and then with the cell, and it read the cell's words and nothing else: at a rule, a formula, a described picture or text a book hides from the page and keeps for assistive technology it stopped in silence moving by line, took a step that said nothing moving by word, and read on past it. The cell made a new accessibility element for each of them every time VoiceOver read its text and kept none, and VoiceOver asks an element what it is after that read, when the element was gone. A cell now keeps its elements, and each is what it is in the text around the table: VoiceOver says "Separator" at a rule, a picture's or a formula's words and "image", a hidden label's words, and "image" alone at a picture nobody describes; decoration is still passed over, and a table inside a cell is read as its cells' words. A picture's element offers Inspect Image. A rule still has no words for a copy, a selection or a search, the cell's characters and ranges are unchanged, and nothing changes on iPhone or iPad.
- Turn the page from VoiceOver's rotor on iPhone and iPad while text is selected. A line's own Next Page and Previous Page did nothing while text was selected, for instance after Select Table Text, and reported success all the same; the pair that the reader's SwiftUI surface named was the one that still turned the page then, and iPhone and iPad no longer have it. A page turn chosen in the rotor is a command, as a host's is, so selected text no longer holds it back, and it now reports failure at either end of the book. An item with no action of its own, as a line of continuous scroll is, gives VoiceOver no list of actions where it gave an empty one.
- Move by heading through the whole book in paging on Mac. A page's Headings rotor held that page's headings alone, so VoiceOver's move to the next heading (Control-Option-Command-H) said "Heading not found" on most pages, and the Heading menu of its rotor was empty or held one item, where continuous scroll and the iPhone and iPad reader already went on through the book. A page's rotor now holds every heading of the book, by its words and level. Moving to one on another page, with the command or by choosing it from the menu, turns to its page and leaves VoiceOver's cursor on the heading; VoiceOver then reads that page from its top, as it does after any page turn. From a page VoiceOver has not read into, the move goes to the first heading from the top of that page. Opening the rotor turns no page. For the few seconds a book takes to build after it opens, the menu holds the chapters built so far. Continuous scroll, iPhone and iPad are unchanged ([#397](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/397)).
- Offer a line of the text its own actions and no others to VoiceOver on iPhone and iPad. VoiceOver said "Actions available" after every line of continuous scroll, and its rotor offered Previous Page and Next Page there, in a flow that has no pages. In a book with links or pictures it also offered, on every line, the links of the whole book by their words and Inspect Image for each picture on screen. On a page a plain line offered Next Page and Previous Page twice, with the inspection of any picture on the page between them. UIKit gives VoiceOver the actions of everything that holds an element along with the element's own. The reader's SwiftUI surface named the two page actions in every flow, which SwiftUI puts on the canvas, and a text view that lists its lines added UIKit's actions for the links of its text and the reader's for its pictures. On iPhone and iPad the surface now names none and such a text view offers none, so a line offers what is in it: Next Page and Previous Page on a page, once; Inspect Image on a picture's own line; its cells' actions on a table's row; and nothing on a line of plain text or a link in continuous scroll, after which VoiceOver says no "Actions available". On Mac, where a text area's lines have no actions of their own, the surface's Next Page and Previous Page are as they were.
- Tell VoiceOver on Mac that a rule is a separator, where it said nothing. An `<hr>` between two paragraphs, as a book writes a change of scene or subject, was a character of the text area with nothing for VoiceOver to say: moving by line it stopped on the rule's line in silence, reading on by itself it went from one scene into the next, and on a page that holds a rule alone it said "In text" and then "end of text". It now says "Separator" at a rule, moving by line or by word and reading on, in pages and in continuous scroll. The word is the reader's own, as on iPhone and iPad, and English whatever the language of VoiceOver or of the book. AppKit has no role for a break in content: at its splitter role, which is what a web view gives an `hr` on Mac, VoiceOver said "horizontal splitter collapsed on top", and with the web view's own role description "horizontal separator no action available collapsed on top". A rule still has no words for selections, Copy or search, and the text area's value and ranges are unchanged. A rule in a table cell is still not announced ([#417](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/pull/417)).
- Read back up the screen when VoiceOver comes into continuous scroll from below on iPhone and iPad. A VoiceOver user on a control below the reader who moved back into the text heard "Vertical scroll bar", at best the last line on screen, and then the control before the text, anywhere but at the top of the book. VoiceOver is given the last line on screen as its cursor comes to that control and asks for the line above it one move later, and in between it has UIKit bring the scroll bar into view, which takes the reader 88 points back: the line was then below the screen and no longer listed, and UIKit answered with the control before the text. The scroll view now keeps that line listed, with the lines between it and the screen, while VoiceOver's cursor is below the text and the view has gone back no further than that move, so VoiceOver reads the scroll bar, the last line that was on screen and then each line above it, on past the top of the screen. After a longer move back, or a move forward, the list ends at the last line on screen as before. The reader still moves as VoiceOver comes in: back as its cursor reaches the scroll bar and forward again as it reaches the line. From the scroll bar itself, reached by a touch or after the book's last line, VoiceOver still goes back to the control before the text and forward to the first line on screen ([#367](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/367)).
- Show an SVG's fallback text where the bundled renderer would copy or read again more of its shared and inherited values than a fixed ceiling allows. The renderer copies a gradient's stops for every shape the gradient paints, stores a dash array for every stroked shape that has or inherits one, parses a long inherited value again for every shape, and clones a text run's style, its font family and `font-variation-settings` with it, for every span, which can be every character. Neither the byte limit nor the node limit caught that: a drawing of a few hundred kilobytes could make the renderer allocate hundreds of megabytes or parse for most of a minute. Each copy is now charged per shape. The counting takes time that grows with the drawing's bytes and has a budget of steps, and a drawing that spends the budget shows fallback text too. The one kind of drawing known to spend it is built to: a `<style>` nested inside another, whose CSS is read again at each level. As measured with a sheet built to be read eight times, that took megabytes of CSS two or three levels deep, or about 140 KB at sixty; these are measurements, not least sizes. What a stylesheet's declarations cost the renderer is charged only in part, so a drawing of a few kilobytes can still pass and cost it hundreds of megabytes ([#282](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/282)). Of 1,453 SVGs from a Mac's system and application folders, none changes verdict ([#252](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/252)).
- Read a table once to VoiceOver on iPhone and iPad, each row where it stands among the lines of its page. In pages VoiceOver read every cell on the page first, each as an item of its own, before the page's first line, and then read each row again as an image; in continuous scroll it read the rows alone, as images. The text views that take touches over the cells were left to VoiceOver as containers of their text, and it searches a page column the canvas lists to it before it takes the column's own lines. Those text views are now hidden from assistive technology, and a row is text, read as its cells' words, in both flows. A row offers the actions of its own cells (select a cell's text, follow a link in it, inspect an image in it) where every line of a page offered those of every cell on it; a row of pictures without words is read as an image, and a row that shows nothing, or decoration alone, is no longer a stop ([#356](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/356)).
- Read on past the screen in continuous scroll on iPhone and iPad when VoiceOver comes into the text straight after a menu closes. At its first move after a menu closes VoiceOver asks for the next fifty items at once and reads through those before it asks again. The scroll view listed its lines on screen and no others, so after choosing a chapter from the contents VoiceOver read to the last line that had been on screen and went on to the scroll bar and the host's next control; an iPad on its side did so, and an iPhone simulator after any menu. The scroll view now lists the fifty lines after the screen as well, and fifty each side of the screen while VoiceOver's cursor is on a line, so it reads on through the book in either direction. It still comes in at the first line on screen and nothing scrolls, and moving back into the text from below still meets the last line on screen. A UI test's snapshot of the reader now holds the fifty lines below the screen too ([#339](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/339)).
- Name every link for VoiceOver on Mac, reach one that is a hidden label, and announce a link that is a picture or a hidden label as VoiceOver reads. VoiceOver finds the links of a text with Find Next Link (Control-Option-Command-L), says each one's name and "hyperlink", and follows it with Control-Option-Space. The names were AppKit's, taken from the characters, so a link that is a picture was "hyperlink" with no name, whatever its `alt` said, and a link that is a hidden label, as a Standard Ebooks page's "skip" links are, was not reached at all: it takes no room, and its element had no size. The reader now gives VoiceOver link elements of its own, in pages and in continuous scroll. A link is called by its words and by the words of its pictures, hidden labels and formulas, and a link that is only a picture says so: "Described jump, image hyperlink", or "image hyperlink" where the picture has no words. Such a link is also announced where VoiceOver reads it, by word, by line and in Read All: "hyperlink Parked link to the target", where it read the label and said nothing of a link. A link of words alone is still read as its words, as in every text view on the Mac, where VoiceOver announces a link in text only at an attachment; it is found, named and followed as before. A picture in a link is announced as a link that is an image, where it was an image, and keeps its Inspect Image action. Recorded with real VoiceOver on a hosted runner; iPhone and iPad are unchanged.
- Tell VoiceOver which lines of a book are headings, and let it move through the book by them. `h1` to `h6`, and elements given `role="heading"`, were read as plain text: nothing marked them, so VoiceOver's Headings rotor found none in any book and a listener could not tell a chapter title from the sentence after it. On iPhone and iPad a heading is now one item with the heading trait and its level, read whole however many lines it takes, and with the rotor on Headings a flick moves to the next or previous heading of the book, turning the page or scrolling to it. On Mac the heading's text carries its level, VoiceOver's move to the next heading works in the text, and its rotor lists the headings by their words and level: a page's in paging, the whole book's in continuous scroll. A heading that a book hides from the page and keeps for assistive technology is a heading too. No character, saved position or line of any page is different ([#355](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/355)).
- Tell VoiceOver that a rule is a separator on iPhone and iPad, where it was called an image. An `<hr>` between two paragraphs, as a book writes a change of scene or subject, was listed as an image without a name, and VoiceOver said "Image" at each one, in pages and in continuous scroll. HTML makes the element a break between the paragraphs it parts and gives it the separator role. UIKit has no trait for one, so the rule's stop now carries the reader's own word: VoiceOver stops between the two paragraphs and says "Separator", in English whatever its language, as it says the names of the page actions. A page that holds a rule alone is turned from that stop. A rule still has no words for selections, Copy or search. `role="presentation"` and `aria-hidden` on a rule are not read: such a rule is announced like any other ([#386](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/pull/386)).
- Match a subsequent-sibling selector (`~`) over a long run of siblings in work that grows with the run, not with its square. Every earlier sibling was walked for every element that asked, so 4,000 paragraphs under two such rules tested 16 million compound selectors; they now test 16 thousand, 24 thousand at worst. What each search found is remembered while a section is built, one entry for each `~` in a rule and each parent it is asked under, up to 65,536 entries. Past that the cost is the old square again, with a lookup for each search besides: about a tenth more work than before where it was measured ([#341](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/341)). A sheet with no `~` costs about one percent more to style each element. Nothing matches differently ([#112](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/112)).
- Pass over an image that a book marks as decoration, and stop calling an image without alternative text "Book image". HTML tells an empty `alt` from a missing one, and the reader read both to VoiceOver as "Book image": a Standard Ebooks title page, which keeps its title and author as hidden text beside `<img alt="">`, was read as the title, the author and then "Book image", or, where the page's picture is an SVG with a title of its own, as that title after them. An image with an empty `alt` now has no stop, no name and no Inspect Image action for VoiceOver on iPhone, iPad and Mac, in pages and in continuous scroll, and a line with such an image in it is read as one line. An image with no `alt` at all is still a stop: VoiceOver says that it is an image, in its own word and language, and the reader makes up no name for it, there or in the image inspector. A link stays reachable whatever its image's `alt` says: a decorative image that is all its link holds is kept, as an image link without a name. On iPhone and iPad a page or a screen that holds decoration alone still lists it, since VoiceOver there turns the page and reads on from an element of the text. Selections, Copy and search take an image's words as before, with one difference: an SVG file's own title no longer stands for an image the book gave an empty `alt`, so it is not copied or found there either ([#354](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/354)).
- Finish laying out a section in which a tab has no stop to reach. TextKit cannot place a tab that has neither a tab stop after it nor a default tab interval: it ended the paragraph at the tab, so the rest was not drawn, or, in a section with a float or another box, asked for the next line down without end, and a section of about 120 bytes kept the reader laying it out for ever. Such a tab came after an outside list marker whose line starts past its stop, from an inline run that keeps its tabs (`white-space: pre`, `pre-wrap` or `break-spaces`), in a string marker, between the cells of a table that flows as text, and in a `pre` block whose font's space has no width. Every paragraph now has a default tab interval of at least a point: eight spaces of its font, or the marker's width in the paragraph an outside list marker opens. CSS `tab-size` is not read ([#378](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/378)), and the advance after a tab between the cells of a table that flows as text can be under a space wide ([#334](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/334)). A table cell that starts with such a tab is no longer measured as having no width, which starved its column, and a `nowrap` cell with tabs is now scaled down, where it was shown at full size only by breaking its line at every tab. A paragraph that holds no tab is laid out as it was ([#227](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/227)).
- Draw the paragraph after floats, positioned boxes or a sized inline block at the top of a page or section with each of its lines on a row of its own. A box takes no room in the flow, and a rounding difference could start the paragraph after it a hair above the top, where TextKit puts every line after the first on one row. A page whose first box was pushed well below the top can still stack its lines ([#242](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/242)); four of 128,386 generated pages that the rounding fault had kept clear of that now show it, with less of their text than before. The fixture and example books are laid out as they were ([#146](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/146)).
- Keep the control strip's page buttons and search field on screen with the keyboard up at larger text sizes on iPhone and iPad. `EPUBReaderControlStrip` was as tall as its groups whatever it was offered. Its own field raises the keyboard and kept it up after a search, and the search adds the match row, so in a host that stacks the strip with the reader the stack could be taller than the room above the keyboard. It was then centred on that room and its top went off the screen: on an iPhone on its side the page buttons were above the screen after a search from the largest ordinary text size up, and at the largest accessibility size 9 of the field's 73 points were left; an iPad mini on its side and an upright iPhone SE lost their page buttons at that size. The strip now scrolls its groups where it is offered less height than they need, and is what it was where it has the room. It sets a layout priority of 1 on itself so that a stack offers it its height before the reader; a host that pads the strip sets the priority again after the padding, which hides it ([reader controls](reader-controls.md)). The search field, in the strip and in a search pane, also gives up focus there when Return or its Search button submits a search, which puts the keyboard away so that the page or the results the search shows are not under it. Clear Search still returns focus to the field, and the Mac is unchanged ([#330](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/330)).
- Start VoiceOver at a page's first line when it comes back to a column after a page turn on Mac. VoiceOver keeps a text cursor of its own in a column it has left. Having read down a line or two, left the column and turned the page, it came back to the column at the character offset it had on the page before and read on from there: the third line of the new page where it had reached the third of the old. It did so after Next Page and Previous Page, in one column and in two, however the turn was made. The reader's notice of a new page now comes from the element that holds the columns, on which VoiceOver gives that cursor up: for `EPUBReaderView` that is the group SwiftUI makes of a window's content, and it was heard with the page controls beside the columns and in the window's toolbar. A VoiceOver cursor resting on that group itself, not on anything in it, now moves into the left column and reads it when a page is installed: heard on page turns, and by the code the same for a new text size, a resize or a shown position. A turn made while VoiceOver's cursor rests on the column without interacting still resumes at the old offset. Continuous scroll did not have the fault and is unchanged ([#337](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/337), [#373](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/373)).
- Keep the place in continuous scroll when a bar shows or hides over the reader. A host that runs the reader under its bars changes the height of the reading area, and not the reader's size, when one comes or goes. A book with a full-page title page, dedication or epigraph, or any other box sized from the reading area, was then laid out again and its place not found again. On iPhone a dedication or epigraph stood up to 81 points below the top of the reading area while the navigation bar was hidden. On Mac, once a window's toolbar had been shown or hidden over the reader, the next change of appearance showed another chapter and reported it as the place. The position being read is now put back at the top of the reading area, as after a resize. On iPhone and iPad that moves the text of such a book by the bar's height when a bar hides, where it used to stay where it was ([#338](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/338)).
- Hide a positioned label that a book parks off screen with an `em` or `rem` offset, as one parked with a pixel offset already was, and read every such label to VoiceOver. Standard Ebooks title pages showed the heading and byline that they keep for assistive technology at `left: -999em`, above the image of the same words and pushing it some 275 points down the page at 17-point text, onto a later page at large sizes; imprints and colophons showed a heading above their logo, and each such book carried the notice that box layouts may be simplified. The rule reads the offset as the book wrote it, 16 CSS pixels to the `em`, so `-62.5em` or less hides at every text size and a book's text and saved positions do not change with the size. A hidden label used to be left out of the text altogether, so VoiceOver lost it as well: beside an image with no alternative text it read "Book image" and nothing more. Labels parked off screen or clipped to nothing are now in the text as characters that take no room, which VoiceOver, selections and Copy read as their words, in the line of the content they precede; the page is laid out as before. `visibility: hidden` and `aria-hidden` still hide from everyone. Percentage and viewport offsets are shown as before ([#63](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/63)).
- Open a footnote hidden from the page for a reference to any element inside it. A note was found only by its own id and those of its leading descendants, so a link to a footnote's second paragraph, or to the text after its number, found no note, and since a footnote `aside` is hidden from the page it led nowhere a reader could see. The popover now shows the whole footnote whichever element the link names, and a note that only wraps other notes shows them, where it opened an empty popover. A footnote-typed element inside a formula or a picture is drawn there but counted as hidden, so a reference to a later id inside it opens a popover where it used to navigate ([#344](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/344)). A note that stays on the page is named as before, by its own id and those of its leading descendants, and a reference to any other element in it goes to that element: a link to the seventeenth item of an endnotes list marked as one note goes to that item. A table cell or row marked as a footnote is named as before too ([#177](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/177)).
- Start VoiceOver at the first line on screen in continuous scroll on iPhone and iPad. The scroll view's accessibility elements were UIKit's own, one for every paragraph of the book, so VoiceOver moving into the text from a control took the book's first paragraph and scrolled the reader back to it, wherever the reader was.
