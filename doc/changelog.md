# Release notes

## Unreleased

- Read back up the screen when VoiceOver comes into continuous scroll from below on iPhone and iPad. A VoiceOver user on a control below the reader who moved back into the text heard "Vertical scroll bar", at best the last line on screen, and then the control before the text, anywhere but at the top of the book. VoiceOver is given the last line on screen as its cursor comes to that control and asks for the line above it one move later, and in between it has UIKit bring the scroll bar into view, which takes the reader 88 points back: the line was then below the screen and no longer listed, and UIKit answered with the control before the text. The scroll view now keeps that line listed, with the lines between it and the screen, while VoiceOver's cursor is below the text and the view has gone back no further than that move, so VoiceOver reads the scroll bar, the last line that was on screen and then each line above it, on past the top of the screen. After a longer move back, or a move forward, the list ends at the last line on screen as before. The reader still moves as VoiceOver comes in: back as its cursor reaches the scroll bar and forward again as it reaches the line. From the scroll bar itself, reached by a touch or after the book's last line, VoiceOver still goes back to the control before the text and forward to the first line on screen ([#367](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/367)).
- Read on past the screen in continuous scroll on iPhone and iPad when VoiceOver comes into the text straight after a menu closes. At its first move after a menu closes VoiceOver asks for the next fifty items at once and reads through those before it asks again. The scroll view listed its lines on screen and no others, so after choosing a chapter from the contents VoiceOver read to the last line that had been on screen and went on to the scroll bar and the host's next control; an iPad on its side did so, and an iPhone simulator after any menu. The scroll view now lists the fifty lines after the screen as well, and fifty each side of the screen while VoiceOver's cursor is on a line, so it reads on through the book in either direction. It still comes in at the first line on screen and nothing scrolls, and moving back into the text from below still meets the last line on screen. A UI test's snapshot of the reader now holds the fifty lines below the screen too ([#339](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/339)).
- Tell VoiceOver which lines of a book are headings, and let it move through the book by them. `h1` to `h6`, and elements given `role="heading"`, were read as plain text: nothing marked them, so VoiceOver's Headings rotor found none in any book and a listener could not tell a chapter title from the sentence after it. On iPhone and iPad a heading is now one item with the heading trait and its level, read whole however many lines it takes, and with the rotor on Headings a flick moves to the next or previous heading of the book, turning the page or scrolling to it. On Mac the heading's text carries its level, VoiceOver's move to the next heading works in the text, and its rotor lists the headings by their words and level: a page's in paging, the whole book's in continuous scroll. A heading that a book hides from the page and keeps for assistive technology is a heading too. No character, saved position or line of any page is different ([#355](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/355)).
- Tell VoiceOver that a rule is a separator on iPhone and iPad, where it was called an image. An `<hr>` between two paragraphs, as a book writes a change of scene or subject, was listed as an image without a name, and VoiceOver said "Image" at each one, in pages and in continuous scroll. HTML makes the element a break between the paragraphs it parts and gives it the separator role. UIKit has no trait for one, so the rule's stop now carries the reader's own word: VoiceOver stops between the two paragraphs and says "Separator", in English whatever its language, as it says the names of the page actions. A page that holds a rule alone is turned from that stop. A rule still has no words for selections, Copy or search. On Mac a rule has no element of its own and VoiceOver says nothing at it, as before. `role="presentation"` and `aria-hidden` on a rule are not read: such a rule is announced like any other ([#386](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/pull/386)).
- Pass over an image that a book marks as decoration, and stop calling an image without alternative text "Book image". HTML tells an empty `alt` from a missing one, and the reader read both to VoiceOver as "Book image": a Standard Ebooks title page, which keeps its title and author as hidden text beside `<img alt="">`, was read as the title, the author and then "Book image", or, where the page's picture is an SVG with a title of its own, as that title after them. An image with an empty `alt` now has no stop, no name and no Inspect Image action for VoiceOver on iPhone, iPad and Mac, in pages and in continuous scroll, and a line with such an image in it is read as one line. An image with no `alt` at all is still a stop: VoiceOver says that it is an image, in its own word and language, and the reader makes up no name for it, there or in the image inspector. A link stays reachable whatever its image's `alt` says: a decorative image that is all its link holds is kept, as an image link without a name. On iPhone and iPad a page or a screen that holds decoration alone still lists it, since VoiceOver there turns the page and reads on from an element of the text. Selections, Copy and search take an image's words as before, with one difference: an SVG file's own title no longer stands for an image the book gave an empty `alt`, so it is not copied or found there either ([#354](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/354)).
- Keep the control strip's page buttons and search field on screen with the keyboard up at larger text sizes on iPhone and iPad. `EPUBReaderControlStrip` was as tall as its groups whatever it was offered. Its own field raises the keyboard and kept it up after a search, and the search adds the match row, so in a host that stacks the strip with the reader the stack could be taller than the room above the keyboard. It was then centred on that room and its top went off the screen: on an iPhone on its side the page buttons were above the screen after a search from the largest ordinary text size up, and at the largest accessibility size 9 of the field's 73 points were left; an iPad mini on its side and an upright iPhone SE lost their page buttons at that size. The strip now scrolls its groups where it is offered less height than they need, and is what it was where it has the room. It sets a layout priority of 1 on itself so that a stack offers it its height before the reader; a host that pads the strip sets the priority again after the padding, which hides it ([reader controls](reader-controls.md)). The search field, in the strip and in a search pane, also gives up focus there when Return or its Search button submits a search, which puts the keyboard away so that the page or the results the search shows are not under it. Clear Search still returns focus to the field, and the Mac is unchanged ([#330](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/330)).
- Start VoiceOver at a page's first line when it comes back to a column after a page turn on Mac. VoiceOver keeps a text cursor of its own in a column it has left. Having read down a line or two, left the column and turned the page, it came back to the column at the character offset it had on the page before and read on from there: the third line of the new page where it had reached the third of the old. It did so after Next Page and Previous Page, in one column and in two, however the turn was made. The reader's notice of a new page now comes from the element that holds the columns, on which VoiceOver gives that cursor up: for `EPUBReaderView` that is the group SwiftUI makes of a window's content, and it was heard with the page controls beside the columns and in the window's toolbar. A VoiceOver cursor resting on that group itself, not on anything in it, now moves into the left column and reads it when a page is installed: heard on page turns, and by the code the same for a new text size, a resize or a shown position. A turn made while VoiceOver's cursor rests on the column without interacting still resumes at the old offset. Continuous scroll did not have the fault and is unchanged ([#337](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/337), [#373](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/373)).
- Keep the place in continuous scroll when a bar shows or hides over the reader. A host that runs the reader under its bars changes the height of the reading area, and not the reader's size, when one comes or goes. A book with a full-page title page, dedication or epigraph, or any other box sized from the reading area, was then laid out again and its place not found again. On iPhone a dedication or epigraph stood up to 81 points below the top of the reading area while the navigation bar was hidden. On Mac, once a window's toolbar had been shown or hidden over the reader, the next change of appearance showed another chapter and reported it as the place. The position being read is now put back at the top of the reading area, as after a resize. On iPhone and iPad that moves the text of such a book by the bar's height when a bar hides, where it used to stay where it was ([#338](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/338)).
- Hide a positioned label that a book parks off screen with an `em` or `rem` offset, as one parked with a pixel offset already was, and read every such label to VoiceOver. Standard Ebooks title pages showed the heading and byline that they keep for assistive technology at `left: -999em`, above the image of the same words and pushing it some 275 points down the page at 17-point text, onto a later page at large sizes; imprints and colophons showed a heading above their logo, and each such book carried the notice that box layouts may be simplified. The rule reads the offset as the book wrote it, 16 CSS pixels to the `em`, so `-62.5em` or less hides at every text size and a book's text and saved positions do not change with the size. A hidden label used to be left out of the text altogether, so VoiceOver lost it as well: beside an image with no alternative text it read "Book image" and nothing more. Labels parked off screen or clipped to nothing are now in the text as characters that take no room, which VoiceOver, selections and Copy read as their words, in the line of the content they precede; the page is laid out as before. `visibility: hidden` and `aria-hidden` still hide from everyone. Percentage and viewport offsets are shown as before ([#63](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/63)).
- Start VoiceOver at the first line on screen in continuous scroll on iPhone and iPad. The scroll view's accessibility elements were UIKit's own, one for every paragraph of the book, so VoiceOver moving into the text from a control took the book's first paragraph and scrolled the reader back to it, wherever the reader was. The scroll view now lists its lines on screen, as a page does, with a link or image in a line as an element of its own: VoiceOver comes in at the first line of the reading area and nothing scrolls. Moving on from the last line on screen, or back from the first, it goes on through the book and the view follows, a line at a time where it used to go a paragraph at a time. A heading is read as written, where UIKit read "Chapter one" as "C hapter one". A read of the screen by VoiceOver or a UI test no longer lays out the whole book ([#301](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/301)).
- Keep the place in continuous scroll on a Mac through the scroll view's first display. AppKit aligns a new scroll view's offset to the pixel grid when it first draws it, and the reader took that fraction of a point for a scroll by the person and for a view that had moved. With a legacy scroll bar, which has the text laid out again as it appears, a read of the book by an assistive technology in that moment sent the next Scroll Forward or change to Paging a chapter back; with either scroll bar the reader afterwards kept the start of the shown position's line instead of the position, a line out after a few resizes ([#302](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/302)).
- Keep a full-page dedication or epigraph where it is shown in continuous scroll on Mac when the window's height changes and its width does not. Such a page is shown from its top edge and centres its text in the height of the reading area, so a new height moves its first line. The reader took that for text moved by a layout made behind the view, which it corrects for after VoiceOver has read the book, and scrolled by as much: the page was left above or below the top of the reading area by the change in the blank space over its text, with the page before it reported as the place, on some resizes and not others ([#313](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/313)).
- Keep the search pane's field and Close Search on screen at larger text sizes in an iPad's popover and on a small iPhone held upright. With the keyboard up an iPad mini on its side leaves the popover 284 points and an upright iPhone SE leaves the sheet 387. The result list was promised 160 of them, so at larger text sizes the pane was taller than its container, which cut the top off: at the largest accessibility size on the iPad mini the field, Search and Close Search were above the popover. The list now gives way to 44 points in every height, as it did with the iPhone on its side, and whatever still does not fit under the field scrolls there. A popover with room for its content is the size it was ([#310](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/310)).
- Report the line resting at the top of the reading area as the place in continuous scroll in a book that has a float or positioned box anywhere, which was every Standard Ebooks title while the headings their title pages park off screen counted as boxes. A shown position can rest a fraction of a point low, and the reader took the sliver of the line above it for the first line on screen: the position reported, and saved by a host, was a line early; a dedication or epigraph shown whole was reported as the section before it; and on Mac a window resize moved the text down a line. A line now counts as on screen when the reading area holds its middle, as in a book without such boxes, at the bottom of the area as at the top ([#305](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/305)).
- Set a run of sized inline blocks side by side. A row of asterisks marking an omission, each in a box of fixed width (*Tristram Shandy* in Standard Ebooks' edition), had every box a line below the one before, with the prose after it wrapped round each one. A run now fills its line and goes on at the start of the next, the prose after it goes on from its last box, and a box that opens a paragraph starts that paragraph's line instead of following the last line of the paragraph before. Text, offsets and CFIs are unchanged.
- Keep prose clear of floats, positioned boxes and inline blocks far into a book in continuous scroll. The boxes' places come from a layout of the whole book, and TextKit only estimates the height of the text above the screen, so the two could be screens apart: prose was drawn over the boxes beside it, and a tap on a box found other text. A line or table row that starts exactly where a float ends is no longer set beside it, which put a page's last line below the page ([#147](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/147)).
- Keep the search pane's field and Close Search on screen at larger text sizes with the iPhone on its side. Clear Search and the match navigator grow with the text, and from the second size above the default the pane was taller than the room above the keyboard; its sheet then cut the top off, and at the largest accessibility size the field and the only way to close the pane were off the screen. Whatever does not fit under the field now scrolls there, and scrolling it puts the keyboard away ([#291](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/291)).
- Show a full-page dedication, epigraph or title page as composed in continuous scroll from any position inside it. Only a position at the box's first line rested the box's top edge at the top of the reading area; any other rested its own line there, with the blank space the box keeps above its text scrolled away. Turning back to such a page in paging and then changing to continuous scroll showed the dedication's last line at the top of the screen, and so did a search result on a later line of an epigraph. A position in a flex or grid box with a `height` or `min-height` that fits the reading area now shows the whole box; in a taller box it still rests its own line.
- Keep paragraph margins, centring, right alignment and justification in a book that has a float, a positioned box or a sized inline block anywhere. The reader places every line of such a book itself, and there TextKit aligned a line across the whole column instead of between its paragraph's indents while the right indent was applied twice. In continuous scroll, where that is the whole book, a Standard Ebooks dedication sat right of centre, an epigraph wrapped early with its attribution out in the margin, and so did any indented, centred or right-aligned paragraph; beside a float, prose with its own left margin started past the float instead of at its edge ([#63](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/63), [#150](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/150)).
- Start VoiceOver at the first line on screen in continuous scroll on Mac. It entered the text at the insertion point, which was at the end of the book, so it read the book's last line and then "end of text" wherever the reader was. The insertion point is now on the first visible line after the book opens, after navigation, a scroll step or a change from paging, and after scrolling, unless a click or VoiceOver itself has placed it; a selection is never moved. After the reader's own moves VoiceOver also starts there on coming back to the text, where it used to return to the line it had last read and scroll the view back to it. A note's popover starts at its first line too.
- Keep opening a book that other bytes follow, however many, when they hold no ZIP end record's signature: a page a download was wrapped in, but not a second archive, whose end record is the one read. When more follow than a ZIP comment could hold, the archive is found by one search in memory, provided the file begins as an archive does, and is read as it would be alone; the book's identity and `archiveData` are the whole file's. Read a deflated entry by its stream whatever uncompressed size its directory states, and hold it to the import limits by what it inflates to: an entry declared larger than a limit that inflates within it now opens, and one that inflates past a limit is refused as it does. A compressed size that runs past the end of the archive no longer refuses the book either, unless it is 2^63 bytes or more or a data descriptor follows the entry ([#219](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/219)).
- Keep the place in continuous scroll after an assistive technology has read the book. VoiceOver and a UI test's snapshot ask where every paragraph is, which lays the whole text out behind the scroll view; the next Scroll Forward then reported a position about a chapter back from the text on screen, and changing to Paging showed that place. On Mac the step itself drew the earlier text.
- State a refused search once. A query longer than 512 characters printed its refusal twice in the search pane and three times in the control strip; the field and the strip now leave the command error out while the failed search says the same thing ([#49](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/49)).
- Keep the search pane's field where it is on iPhone. As a sheet the pane was centred, so the field sat in the middle with no results, jumped to the top when results arrived and back on Clear Search; with the iPhone on its side and the keyboard up, results pushed the field and Close Search off the top of the screen. The pane now fills its sheet from the top and its list gives way in a compact height ([#51](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/51)).
- Release a file that is refused because its ZIP end record is cut short. ZIPFoundation kept the whole input for the life of the process when it refused an archive whose end record gave a comment longer than the bytes after it, so a host offered many such files lost the size of each; opening now refuses them itself, with the same error, before ZIPFoundation is asked ([#217](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/217)).
- Keep each occurrence of a repeated spine item's own position. A bookmark, highlight or reported location in the second or third repeat named the first, so restoring it went back there. Positions saved by foliate-js still resolve ([#108](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/108)).
- Page through a book in which no spine item is linear. Next Page was disabled at the end of its first section in paging, though continuous scroll showed the whole book ([#159](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/159)).
- Report progression in a book in which no spine item is linear. Every position and search result came without one, so a host's reading progress stayed empty though the book read through ([#232](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/232)).
- Stop taking the language of a content document that declares none from the package's `dc:language` for generated text: the marks around `q` and locale-sensitive case mapping follow only `lang` and `xml:lang` in the content, so an unlabelled French book gets “ ” around `q`, not « ». The package's language remains a hint for glyph choice and speech ([#109](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/109)).
- Text extraction's recovery keeps text it used to lose: after a `<` that opens no tag (including before `_` and `:`), after an early `</body>` or `</html>`, and around a NUL character ([#93](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/93), [#198](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/198)). The integration guide says what a host that writes resources to disk can rely on in a resource path ([#205](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/205)).
- Lay out a section of many inset positioned boxes in time that grows with the square of their count, not the cube. A chapter of 1,200 margin notes took over a minute and a half to lay out in a debug build and now takes 3 seconds; nothing is placed differently ([#180](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/180)).
- Pin MathMLLayout 0.1.1, whose license file names Vocaro Technologies LLC as its third-party notice does ([#69](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/69)).
- Open books with faults that used to refuse them. A reference that starts with `/` or climbs past the container root resolves at the root, and a query is dropped. A remote manifest item is left out and never fetched. A contents entry whose link does not resolve keeps its title without a target. White space around the `mimetype`, a container that lists another rendition first, a colon in a file name, a manifest href cased differently from its file, `&nbsp;` or a lower-case doctype in a navigation document, and an Adobe-obfuscated font whose key is hard to find or missing are all read past ([#84](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/84), [#171](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/171)).
- Refuse a file that is not a ZIP archive at once instead of after about a fifth of a second per mebibyte, a minute or more at the 256 MiB limit: the archive's end record is looked for in the last 65,557 bytes, where one with the longest comment begins, and no further back unless the file begins as an archive does ([#194](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/194)).
- Keep the text after an unescaped `<` in a section the HTML parser reads. `if x < y then done` was shown as `if x`, and `p <.05` took the rest of its paragraph with it; such signs are now text, as HTML reads them, and style sheets, scripts and attribute values that hold one are unchanged ([#103](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/103)).
- Read an archive entry's name as UTF-8 when its bytes are UTF-8, whether or not the ZIP language-encoding flag is set. `zip` and `ditto` on a Mac leave the flag clear, so a book they archived with a non-ASCII file name was refused when the name was a chapter's, the package document's or an obfuscated font's, and opened without the file otherwise. Names that are not UTF-8 are still read as code page 437 ([#73](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/73)).
- Refuse a book whose ZIP directory declares a stored entry larger than the archive could hold, before anything is read. A 2 KB archive claiming a terabyte for a stored entry kept opening busy for seconds, and one claiming the largest ZIP64 size never finished ([#175](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/175)).
- Open ZIP64 archives whose extra fields hold a zero: the first entry's header offset, which crashed opening, and an empty entry's sizes, which refused the book as over its limits ([#72](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/72)).
- Draw a page that opens just below a float, absolute box or sized inline-block as wide as its column. Its lines were laid out above the page's top edge, twice the box's height up, so the page's first lines were not shown ([#145](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/145)).
- Place each float or positioned box below the boxes before it in one sorted pass instead of rescanning them after every collision. A section of 1,000 floats with nothing between them took 15 seconds to lay out and now takes a quarter of a second; layout is unchanged ([#155](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/155)).
- Decode a percent-encoded container rootfile path. A book whose `container.xml` wrote a space or other character of `full-path` as an escape (`My%20Books/book.opf`) was refused as missing its package document, and would have had fallback CFI bases. The path is now resolved as a URL from the container root and checked after decoding, and read as written when only that names an entry ([#81](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/81)).
- Draw the image of a single-image SVG wrapper filling its box when the `viewBox` is so small, or so far from the image, that the box would show less than a pixel of it, and size an SVG whose `viewBox` proportions overflow from its image or drawing instead. Both crashed the reader; image attachments now always fit their line and page ([#135](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/135)).
- Refuse an EPUB whose ZIP end record, directory record or data descriptor points past the end of the archive with `EPUBPublicationError.invalidArchive`, including offsets and sizes held in ZIP64 fields. Such a file trapped the host process inside ZIPFoundation's in-memory reader instead of failing to open ([#71](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/71)).
- Show sections whose text has an unescaped `<` followed later by an apostrophe or quotation mark, such as `if x < y then it's done` or a style sheet's `content: "<"`. The declaration screen took the sign for a tag and the mark for an unclosed string, and withheld the section instead of reading it with the HTML parser. Refuse a `[` directly after a DOCTYPE's closing `>`, which libxml2 reads as an internal subset ([#87](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/87)).
- Show the whole book in continuous scroll again after a text size or appearance is changed and changed back before its rebuild finished. The scroll view stayed on the current section, so the rest of the book could not be reached until a navigation command reloaded it ([#156](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/156)).
- Show a footnote whose reference names the paragraph inside it. A footnote `aside` with no id of its own was hidden from the flow without being captured, so its note could not be read at all; a note that opens with another note no longer takes that note's id, which left the inner note's text nowhere ([#126](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/126)).
- Read an XHTML element named only with colons (`<:/>`) in text extraction instead of trapping. XML 1.0 admits the name and libxml2 hands it on in both strict and recovery modes; it is now an unknown inline element that keeps its text and ID ([#88](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/88)).
- Show the inline search field's queued line only for a queued search. It appeared for every command, briefly resizing inline controls and the reader under them, so on iPhone and iPad the first Next Page press after opening a book landed back on the same page ([#28](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/28)).
- Leave focus alone when the control strip's inline search field appears. It focused itself as a search pane's field does, so opening a book on iPhone raised the keyboard over most of the page and started VoiceOver in an editing field ([#28](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/28)).
- Give VoiceOver an iPad spread's columns in source order. A chapter opening's left column, lowered by its top spacing, was read after the right column ([#28](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/28)).
- Break MathML formulas wider than their line into full-size rows at relations, operators and authored `linebreak`s instead of shrinking them below three quarters of the text's size; disclose the ones no row can keep readable. Show `mglyph` alternatives in place and set `mstack` and `mlongdiv` as digit-column tables, each disclosed. Read `alttext` in the content's language and tag generated English readings as English ([#25](https://github.com/vocaro/EPUBLib/issues/25)).
